    How it's made

    Land: Copernicus GLO-30 elevation (about 30 m; a surface model, so woods and buildings stand up), resampled to 12.5 m.

    Beach: the sand between the dune foot and the waterline in a cloud-free Sentinel-2 image (4 Sep 2023) becomes a sloping intertidal beach. The dune foot is taken as +3.6 m and that waterline as −1.8 m; both are estimates.

    Seabed: a smooth shoreface with two sandbars on sandy coast (steeper off rock), blended over the first 0.6–1.8 km into the coarse depths in AWS Terrain Tiles. The inner surf zone is therefore approximate.

    Waves: the forecast's main swell is traced across that seabed by solving for its crest pattern, so it turns towards shallow water, grows as it slows and breaks at 0.78 of the depth. A second swell, when forecast, is added by energy to the breaking heights but not drawn. There is no diffraction, wind or current, so the lee of a headland comes out quieter than it really is.

    Forecast: Open-Meteo marine and weather models at a point offshore. Its sea level includes the tide but is less reliable near coasts.
